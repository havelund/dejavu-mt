
- Timed operators (S[<=n], P[<=n], P[>n], H[<=n]…) — the biggest 
expressiveness showcase left. DejaVu needs bit-cube time encodings; 
in our setting time is just an Int in the formula — arguably the most 
elegant demonstration of the SMT approach, and paper-worthy.

- Recursive rules (where … :=) — completes DejaVu feature parity, moderate 
effort.

- Seen-only lowercase exists/forall — small, bounded scope.

- Compact live-set encoding — the real research problem (the 67s-vs-0.1s gap). 
Hard; probably a paper of its own.

- The two builds tell one story. GC closed the dead-term half of the growth problem cheaply; 
what remains (the access-log gap) is purely the live-set representation. That's now a 
crisply posed research question: what is the formula-world analogue of a BDD's 
shared, canonical set encoding? Candidates worth mulling: interval/range predicates 
when values are ordered (5 ≤ u ≤ 5000 instead of 5000 disjuncts — the access 
log would collapse to exactly this), or a hybrid where predicate nodes store 
explicit value sets and only theory-relevant nodes hold formulas.

- The QE-over-EUF limitation we found (Z3's qe2 refusing uninterpreted sorts) is a 
genuinely citable observation — it explains why a formula-based DejaVu can't 
simply use the theory of equality, which a reviewer would otherwise propose 
in one line.
